If you're new to football (soccer), looking at a league table for the first time can look like a wall of confusing acronyms. P, W, D, L, GF, GA, GD, Pts... what does it all mean?

Here is a complete breakdown of how football league points work and how standings are calculated.

## The 3-1-0 Point System

Almost every major football league in the world (including the Premier League, La Liga, and the World Cup group stages) uses the **three points for a win** system.

- **Win (W)**: 3 Points
- **Draw (D)**: 1 Point
- **Loss (L)**: 0 Points

This system was widely adopted in the 1980s and 90s to encourage attacking football. Under the old "two points for a win" system, teams were often content to defend and settle for a draw. By increasing the reward for a win to three points, teams are highly incentivized to risk attacking for the victory.

## Understanding the Acronyms

To calculate the points, we need to track several other metrics:

- **Played (P or PL)**: The number of matches the team has participated in so far.
- **Goals For (GF)**: The total number of goals the team has scored across all matches.
- **Goals Against (GA)**: The total number of goals the team has conceded.
- **Goal Difference (GD)**: The calculation of `GF - GA`. This is arguably the most important metric after points.

## How Tie-Breakers Work

If a league runs for 38 games, it is highly likely that two or more teams will finish on the exact same number of points. How do we decide who wins the league?

Different competitions have different rules, but the two most common are:

### 1. The Goal Difference Method (e.g., Premier League, World Cup)
If teams are tied on points, the team with the higher **Goal Difference (GD)** is placed higher. 
If they have the exact same Goal Difference, the team with the most **Goals For (GF)** wins the tie. 
If they are still tied, it goes to head-to-head records.

### 2. The Head-to-Head Method (e.g., La Liga, Champions League)
In Spain and in UEFA competitions, if teams are tied on points, their overall Goal Difference is ignored. Instead, the league looks exclusively at the matches played *between the tied teams*. Whoever scored the most points (or goals) in those specific matches wins the tie-breaker.

## Managing Your Own League

If you are setting up a local 5-a-side or Sunday League, we highly recommend sticking to the standard Goal Difference tie-breaker, as it's the easiest for players to understand and track.
