A well-implemented sales leaderboard can transform a sluggish sales floor into a highly productive, high-energy environment. However, a poorly implemented one can foster resentment and toxic competition.

Here is how to create a sales leaderboard that drives the *right* kind of motivation.

## 1. Track Behaviors, Not Just Revenue

The most common mistake managers make is only tracking total closed revenue. 

While revenue is the ultimate goal, it is a lagging indicator. If a junior SDR sees that the senior Account Executive is $50,000 ahead of them on day two of the month, they will immediately give up.

Instead, create leaderboards that track **leading indicators**—the daily behaviors that eventually lead to revenue:
- Number of outbound calls made.
- Number of meetings booked.
- Discovery calls completed.

By gamifying the grind, you reward hard work and give junior reps a fair chance to claim the #1 spot.

## 2. Make it Highly Visible

A leaderboard hidden in an Excel file buried in a shared drive is completely useless. 

[CTA:Default]

To create a culture of transparency and friendly competition, your leaderboard must be visible at all times. 
- **In the Office**: Cast the leaderboard to the big TV screens on the sales floor.
- **For Remote Teams**: Share the live link in your team's Slack channel and pin it to the top. Make it a habit to review the top 3 climbers during your morning standups.

## 3. Celebrate Milestones, Not Just the Winner

If only the person in 1st place gets recognition, the other 90% of your team will feel undervalued. 

Use your leaderboard software to set visual target lines or quotas. When a rep crosses that threshold, celebrate it! Throw digital confetti on the screen, ring a physical bell in the office, and make sure their achievement is recognized.

By focusing on personal milestones rather than just absolute rankings, you encourage reps to compete against their own past performance, creating a healthier and more productive sales culture.
